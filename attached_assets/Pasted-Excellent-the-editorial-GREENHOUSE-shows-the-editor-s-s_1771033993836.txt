**What's missing or worrying me:**

**I can't leave notes on a piece.** When I'm browsing the Garden Stream and I read something that interests me but I'm not ready to act on, I need to write myself a note. "Strong opening, ending falls apart — check back in a month." "Reminds me of the piece we ran last spring — could pair well." The Greenhouse shortlist lets me save and prioritise, but there's no visible space for editorial annotations. Without that, I'm going to forget *why* I saved something, and that defeats the purpose.

**There's no way to see a writer's full body of work from the Stream.** I see "Field Notes from the Unbuilt House" by sophia, and it stops me. Great. Now I want to see everything sophia has written — not just what's marked READY TO SHOW, but the seeds, the growing pieces, the dormant ones. Because editorial instinct isn't just about one piece. It's about voice. I want to know if this writer has a consistent thing happening or if this was a one-off. The Stream shows me individual pieces. I need a quick path to the writer's entire garden.

**The "ADD TO GREENHOUSE" button is the only editorial action on a piece.** When I'm reading in the Stream, I need more granularity than save or don't save. I want to be able to: mark it as "watching" (I'll check back), leave a private editorial note, send a quiet signal to the writer ("an editor paused here"), or immediately draft a publish request. Right now it's binary — add to my Greenhouse or move on. Real editorial work has more shades than that.

**There's no collaboration between editors.** If I'm not the only editor (and any journal worth its salt has more than one), I need to see what my colleagues have saved, what they've noted, whether two of us independently flagged the same piece. The Greenhouse appears to be personal. There's no shared editorial space. In a real editorial workflow, the best moments happen when one editor says "you need to read this" to another. That handoff doesn't exist here.

**Overall as an editor:**

The bones are right. The philosophy — go to the writers instead of making them come to you — genuinely solves the worst part of my job, which is drowning in a slush pile where 90% of submissions are wildly wrong for the journal. If writers are already writing in gardens, and I can browse by genre and stage, I'm only ever reading work that's at least in the right neighbourhood. That alone saves me dozens of hours per reading period.

But the tool is currently built for the *happy path* — I find something, I love it, I save it, I request it. Real editorial work is messier than that. It's "maybe," "not yet," "come back to this," "show this to Sarah," "this writer isn't ready but they will be," "this piece would be perfect if the third section were cut." The system needs to accommodate the editorial grey zone, not just the yes/no endpoints. Right now it's a discovery tool. It needs to become a *deliberation* tool.

If it got there, I'd switch from Submittable tomorrow. Not because Submittable is terrible — it's just built for the wrong end of the process. It's built for receiving. This is built for finding. And finding is what editors actually want to do.
