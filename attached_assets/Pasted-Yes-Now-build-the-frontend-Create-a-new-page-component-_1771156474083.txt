### SCREEN 1: THE ENTRANCE
Display this opening passage (use serif font, generous line-height, dark background):

"You are standing in a narrow corridor. The walls are the color of wet ink.

Somewhere ahead, a metaphor is waiting to show you what it knows.

You did not come here to learn definitions. You came here because something in your writing has been staying too still — circling the same comparisons, reaching for the same safe distances.

A metaphor is not a decoration. It is a migration.

The image leaves one thing and arrives at another, and in the crossing, both are changed.

Today, we practice letting the image move."

Show a "Continue" button that fades in after 3 seconds.

### SCREEN 2: CRAFT INSIGHT 1
Display:

"WHAT A METAPHOR DOES

Most writing advice treats metaphor as ornamentation — a way to make sentences prettier. But a metaphor is not a brooch pinned to a dress. It is the thread that pulls the fabric into shape.

When you say 'grief is an ocean,' you are not describing grief. You are teaching your body to feel it differently: as something vast, tidal, indifferent to your swimming.

The image does not explain. It relocates.

Every strong metaphor is a small act of transformation. You take one thing and let it travel until it touches something else, and in that touch, both are altered.

This is what we'll practice: not finding metaphors, but following them."

Continue button appears after 2 seconds.

### SCREEN 4: EXERCISE 2 - SYNTAX BLOOM
Display:

"SYNTAX BLOOM

Sentences are organisms. Some sprawl like ivy, covering every surface. Some stay low, like moss — barely there, but persistent. Others rise fast and fall, like wildflowers after rain.

Below, you'll write three versions of the same image. I'll give you a seed: the image of someone waiting.

Write it three ways:
1. MOSS — A short, clipped sentence. Five words or fewer. Let it sit on the page like a held breath.
2. IVY — A long, winding sentence. Let it sprawl. Subordinate clauses, digressions, the whole tangled thing.
3. WILDFLOWER — A balanced sentence. Medium length. It rises, turns, and completes.

Same image. Three different metabolisms."

### SCREEN 5: CRAFT INSIGHT 2
Display:

"ON FIDELITY

There is a kind of loyalty that kills metaphors: the insistence that they 'make sense.'

The most alive comparisons don't explain themselves. They trust the reader's body to understand before the mind catches up.

'Grief is an ocean' works not because grief is actually like an ocean, but because your lungs already know what drowning feels like.

When you follow a metaphor, you are not solving a puzzle. You are agreeing to a temporary belief.

The writer's job is not to justify the comparison — it is to commit to it so fully that the reader forgets it was ever a leap."

Continue button.

### SCREEN 6: EXERCISE 3 - MIGRATION PATH
Display:

"THE MIGRATION PATH

Now we travel.

Write a short paragraph — four to six sentences — in which a metaphor begins as one thing and becomes another by the end.

Don't plan. Start with an image that feels true, and let it shift. The metaphor might begin as weather and end as architecture. It might start as an animal and arrive as a memory.

Your job is to follow it. Not to control it.

Begin."

Show a large text area.
