The editor’s studio can be a dedicated, calm control room that sits on top of the Garden, with a clear flow:

**Garden → Editor Studio → Greenhouse → Issue Builder → Publish to The Page**

Below is a complete breakdown of how it works and what editors can do at each step.

***

## 1. Editor accounts and their Garden

When someone is given editor permissions:

- They get a **standard member account** plus:
  - An **Editor Studio** section.
  - Elevated permissions inside the Garden.
- They automatically have their **own Garden**, like any writer:
  - They can plant their own work.
  - They can participate in Circles.
  - Their writing is discoverable in the members-only Garden like everyone else (no separate “editor class” visually).

Permissions model:

- **Member**: Read and interact with Garden content within visibility rules.
- **Editor**: All of the above, plus:
  - Access to the full Garden reading view (for all pieces marked shareable to the entire Garden).
  - Ability to mark pieces as “shortlisted” (Greenhouse).
  - Ability to send and manage publishing requests.
  - Ability to assemble and publish issues to The Page.

***

## 2. The Editor Studio home

The Editor Studio should feel like a dashboard, not a CMS thicket. One page with clear sections:

1. **Today’s Overview**
   - New Garden pieces since last login.
   - New pieces marked “available for editorial consideration.”
   - Pending author responses to publish requests.
   - Upcoming issue deadlines.

2. **Pipelines**
   - **Garden Stream** (all eligible work).
   - **Greenhouse** (shortlisted).
   - **Requests & Contracts** (in-progress negotiations).
   - **Issues** (draft, scheduled, published).

3. **Search & Filters**
   - Global search by title, author, tags, form, theme.
   - Filters by length, readiness (“ready to show”), date planted, Circle, etc.

Everything important should be reachable from this one page.

***

## 3. Browsing all Garden work (editor view)

Editors need a way to scroll through the whole Garden without breaking the intimacy of the space.

### 3.1 What editors see

Editors see a **Garden reading view** that is:

- Limited to:
  - Pieces marked “Whole Garden” (visible to all members).
  - Pieces marked “Available to editors” (editor visibility toggle).
- Respectful of privacy:
  - Private and Circle-only pieces are not in the editorial pool unless explicitly shared for editorial consideration.

### 3.2 Garden stream layout

For each piece in the editor’s Garden stream, show:

- Title (or “Untitled” if none)
- Author name + quick profile hover (bio, pronouns, time zone, any access notes)
- Tags: form, length, themes
- Readiness: Raw seed / Growing piece / Ready to show
- Availability: Editorial consideration on/off
- Date planted or last updated
- A short excerpt (first few lines)

Actions on each card:

- **Open in reader** (full piece).
- **Add to Greenhouse** (shortlist).
- **View author context** (other pieces, sanctuary journals they like, etc.).
- **Add internal note** (for other editors).

Infinite scroll or paginated, with powerful filters:

- Form (poem, short story, hybrid, essay, etc.).
- Length bands (micro, short, long).
- Thematic tags (trauma, sea, neurodivergence, family, etc.).
- “New since last visit.”
- “Quiet pieces” (few or no reactions).
- “By emerging members” / “By frequent contributors.”

***

## 4. The Greenhouse (shortlist)

The Greenhouse is a private, editor-only shortlist space.

### 4.1 Adding to Greenhouse

From the Garden stream or reader:

- Click **“Add to Greenhouse”**.
- Choose:
  - Which **issue(s)** or **theme folders** it might fit (e.g., “Winter 2026,” “Grief,” “Shared Ground”).
  - Priority (low/medium/high).
  - Optional internal note.

No notification to the writer yet—this is internal thinking space.

### 4.2 Greenhouse view

The Greenhouse view shows:

- All shortlisted pieces, grouped by:
  - Issue theme / folder.
  - Stage (Uncontacted / Request sent / Accepted / Declined).
- Per piece:
  - Author, title, tags, length.
  - Which editor added it; internal notes.
  - Status pill (e.g., “Potential for Issue 4”).

Actions:

- Open piece in reader.
- Move between theme folders.
- Initiate a **publish request**.
- Remove from Greenhouse (piece drops back to the normal Garden state).

***

## 5. Sending publish requests

This is the core interaction between editors and writers: inviting a Garden piece into The Page.

### 5.1 Starting a request

From a piece in the Greenhouse:

- Click **“Request to publish”**.
- Fill out:
  - Target: which **issue** (or “evergreen / online exhibit”).
  - Proposed **publication date / window**.
  - **Notes to author**:
    - Why this piece.
    - Any initial thoughts on edits or layout ideas.
  - **Rights & terms**:
    - Duration of rights.
    - Payment (amount, timing, method).
    - Whether the Garden version stays as-is or gets linked to the final.

This generates a **draft request**.

### 5.2 What the writer sees

The writer receives an in-Garden notification and email:

- Message: “We’d like to invite your piece **[title]** into The Page for [issue/theme].”
- Clear, human language that matches your ethos of invitation and care.
- UI with:
  - Full request message.
  - Rights/payment summary.
  - Proposed timeline.
  - Buttons: **Accept**, **Decline**, **Ask a question**.

The writer can:

- Accept: piece moves into **In contract** state.
- Decline: no hard feelings; the piece remains in their Garden as normal.
- Ask questions: opens a message thread with editors inside the Studio.

### 5.3 Tracking requests

In the Editor Studio, a **Requests & Contracts** tab shows:

Columns:

- Piece
- Author
- Issue / theme
- Status:
  - Draft request (not sent)
  - Sent — awaiting response
  - Accepted — in production
  - Declined
  - Needs contract signature / payment
- Last update date
- Assigned editor

Editors can filter by status, author, issue, and responsible editor.

***

## 6. Issue Builder: assembling The Page

Once pieces are accepted, editors move them into issues.

### 6.1 Issue overview

For each issue (e.g., “Winter 2026,” “Shared Ground”):

- Metadata:
  - Title, subtitle, theme note.
  - Publication date.
  - Status: Draft / Locked / Published.
- Contributions:
  - List of accepted pieces.
  - Layout order (drag-and-drop).
  - Content type (poem, short story, essay, hybrid).
  - Assigned illustrator / designer.

### 6.2 Editing and production

Per piece, editors can:

- Open the **editorial view**:
  - Edit text with version control (track changes).
  - Leave editorial comments for the writer.
  - See writer’s final approved version.
- Attach:
  - Visual concept notes.
  - Illustration briefs.
  - Design references.

Workflow states per piece:

- Draft received
- Edited and approved (text final)
- Design in progress
- Design approved
- Ready to publish

All of this remains internal; the Garden version of the piece remains as the writer first planted it (or minimally updated with a tag like “Exhibited on The Page”).

### 6.3 Publishing to The Page

When an issue is ready:

- Editor hits **“Publish issue”**.
- System:
  - Creates or updates public pages on The Page site for:
    - Issue landing page.
    - Individual piece pages with their illustrated layouts.
  - Connects them to navigation, archive, and contributors page.
- Sends:
  - Author notifications.
  - Optional Garden announcement (for members): “New issue is live; X pieces grew from this Garden.”

***

## 7. Linking Garden and Page

The relationship between internal and public views should be explicit and gentle.

### 7.1 In the Garden

On a piece that has been published:

- A small badge: **“Exhibited on The Page (Issue X)”**.
- Clickable link to the public version.
- History note: “Invited on [date], accepted on [date], published on [date].”

For other members, in the Garden reader:

- They see the badge and can click through to see how a Garden piece became a finished gallery room.

### 7.2 On The Page (public)

On each public piece:

- A subtle line in the contributor note: “This piece grew in The Garden, our private members-only writing space.”
- Link back to a landing page that explains membership and the Garden ecosystem (no direct exposure of internal content).

***

## 8. Roles, safety, and boundaries

Because editors can see a lot, the Studio should bake in safety:

- **Visibility rules enforced in code**:
  - Editors only see pieces where:
    - Visibility is Whole Garden, or
    - Explicitly shared to editors.
  - Private and Circle-only posts stay completely out of the editorial pool unless the writer actively changes visibility.
- **Audit log**:
  - Internal log of which editor viewed or changed what, for accountability and internal trust.
- **Draft vs. final safeguards**:
  - Clear differentiation between:
    - Garden draft version.
    - Edited text.
    - Final published text.
  - No silent overwrites.

***

## 9. Editor experience summary

An editor’s day could look like:

1. Open the Editor Studio.
2. Skim “New to the Garden” and “Newly editor-available” pieces.
3. Read a handful, add promising ones to the Greenhouse.
4. For the upcoming issue, open Greenhouse → select 3–4 pieces → send publish requests.
5. Check the Requests tab, respond to author questions.
6. Move accepted pieces into Issue Builder, finalise edits and design.
7. Publish issue when ready.

They get to operate like curators in a private gallery, not gatekeepers in a slush inbox.

If you want next, the flows above can be turned into concrete screens / sections (e.g., exact fields and controls) you can hand to a designer or dev team.
