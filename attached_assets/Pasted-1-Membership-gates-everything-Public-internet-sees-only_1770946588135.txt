1. Membership gates everything
Public internet sees only: The Page (the journal) + a light marketing site explaining the Garden model.
​

To read or interact with any Garden writing, you must be a logged‑in member.

Inside, members see:

Their Personal Garden

One or more Circles

A Garden Gallery: the wider stream of member work.

Think of it as a private clubhouse: discovery and interaction are rich, but everything is “inside the walls.”

2. Visibility levels for each piece
When planting a piece, a writer chooses one of three scopes:

Private – only me.

Circle‑only – visible to my chosen Circle(s).

Whole Garden – visible to all members and editors.

Plus a simple toggle:

“Available for editorial consideration” (yes/no).

That’s the only “submission” action they ever take. Everything else is just “where in the Garden can this live?”

3. Garden Gallery: discovery without public exposure
Within the members‑only Garden, you can have:

A Garden Feed (chronological + smart ranking):

Only pieces marked “Whole Garden” show.

Filter by tags, form, length, themes.

Optional: “Show me quiet pieces” (no or few responses yet) to encourage attention to overlooked work.

Profile Gardens:

Each member has a Garden profile page listing:

A selection of their Garden pieces (scoped to Whole Garden or Circles you share).

Tiny “inner weather” snippet if they choose (e.g., “currently in fallow season”).

No public follower counts; just “Connections” or “Circles in common” to keep it non‑competitive.

You get discovery and community, but it never leaks beyond membership.

4. Interaction: humane, members‑only
Inside the Garden, members can:

Respond with non‑quantified reactions (Resonated / Held me / Opened something), shown as subtle glows rather than counts.

Leave comments or “Sit with this” acknowledgements.

Invite someone into:

a Circle

a one‑to‑one Compassion Correspondence

a co‑writing session.

All of this is only visible to logged‑in members. There is no way to deep‑link to a piece for non‑members; hitting the URL logged‑out shows a “This lives in the Garden” page + membership invite.

5. Editors’ view inside the same system
Editors use a Garden Curator view that’s basically the same as the Garden Gallery, but with extra tools:

Filters:

Only show pieces where “Available for editorial consideration” = true.

Filter by theme, form, length, tags.

Pin to Greenhouse (shortlist pool).

Invite to The Page:

Flow:

Editor pins a piece from the Garden to the Greenhouse.

If selected, editor sends an in‑app invite: “We’d like to move this from the Garden into the public journal.” (linking clear terms, mirroring your current submission language ).
​

Writer accepts → a public “journal version” is created for the website; the original Garden post remains, tagged “Exhibited on The Page.”

Result: members see each other’s work; readers see only curated pieces on The Page.

6. Mental‑health features in a social Garden
Because everyone is a member, you can safely weave in the mindful tools:

Window of Tolerance:

Before interacting heavily, a writer can set themselves as “quiet mode” (red/blue zone) → their work is visible, but comments queue until they’re ready.

Reframe Engine:

When a piece is un‑pinned from the Greenhouse or a writer logs “disappointed,” they’re offered reframing / self‑compassion prompts privately.

Fallow Field:

Members can mark themselves as in a fallow season. Their profile shows a small icon and the Garden feed may lightly deprioritise their older pieces, so they feel less “on display.”

All still inside the membership wall.

7. How it feels to a member
They join → gain access to the Garden (a private, members‑only reading & writing space).

They plant pieces into:

their own Garden

their Circles

the wider Garden.

They discover others through the Garden Gallery, interact, and form Circles.

Editors quietly wander the same Garden, curate into The Page.

The outside world sees the polished journal and the “this all grew inside a private Garden” story.
