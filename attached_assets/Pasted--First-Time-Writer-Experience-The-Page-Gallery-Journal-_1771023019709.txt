# First-Time Writer Experience: The Page Gallery Journal — Garden UX Review

I went through the entire site as a writer encountering it for the first time. Here's my honest experience and what I'd simplify.

***

## 1. The Landing Page Scroll Animation — Beautiful but Blocking

The homepage opens with a dark starry sky and "SCROLL TO REVEAL". As you scroll, particles slowly form into what looks like text. The problem: **it takes a very long time to get through**, and there's no way to skip it. As a writer who just wants to start writing, I was stuck scrolling through a visual effect with no clear end point. The "SCROLL TO REVEAL" prompt doesn't tell you how far you need to go, and the animation feels more like a tech demo than a front door. [f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit](https://f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit.dev/)

**Recommendation:** Either make the animation much shorter (3-4 scroll ticks max), add a "Skip" option, or replace it with a simple fade-in. The concept is lovely — but it's a wall between the writer and the writing.

***

## 2. The Landing Page Itself — Too Many Sections Saying the Same Thing

The homepage has sections that essentially repeat the same message ("you write, editors find you") in multiple formats: [f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit](https://f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit.dev/)

- The scroll-reveal greeting
- The hero tagline section
- "The Garden" explanation (Seed → Sprout → Bloom)
- "The Two Doors" (Gallery vs Garden comparison)
- "How It Works" (Plant → Editors Walk Through → A Knock on the Door)
- "Why We Do This" manifesto
- A closing tagline

That's **six or seven different ways of explaining one idea** before you even reach the Garden. A first-time writer doesn't need to be convinced this many times. The philosophy is compelling — but it's diluted by repetition.

**Recommendation:** Cut the landing page to three things: (1) one clear tagline, (2) a brief how-it-works (3 steps), and (3) an "Enter Your Garden" button. Move the manifesto and the Two Doors comparison to a separate "About" page for people who want the deeper story.

***

## 3. The Garden Has 8 Tabs — That's Overwhelming

Once inside the garden, the tab bar contains **eight sections** that scroll horizontally: [f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit](https://f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit.dev/garden)

1. **Tables** (community discussions)
2. **Workshop** (writing exercises)
3. **The Desk** (your actual writing)
4. **Swap** (beta reader matching)
5. **The Press** (couldn't load — see bug below)
6. **Rejection Wall** (couldn't load)
7. **Opportunities** (couldn't load)
8. **Idea Drops** (couldn't load)

As a first-time writer, landing on this and seeing a scrollable row of 8 tabs is like walking into a co-working space and being handed a map with 8 rooms before you've even sat down. **I don't know what most of these are, and I don't need most of them on day one.**

**Recommendation:** On first visit, show the writer **only The Desk** (their writing space) with a simple "+ New Piece" prompt. Reveal the other tabs progressively — e.g., after they write their first piece, gently surface Workshop. After their first week, mention Tables. Swap, The Press, Rejection Wall, Opportunities, and Idea Drops should be tucked into a "More" or "Discover" section, not front-and-centre tabs.

***

## 4. Tab Switching Is Buggy

When I scrolled the tab bar and clicked different tabs (THE PRESS, REJECTION WALL, OPPORTUNITIES), the content area didn't change — it stayed stuck on whichever view I was previously in. This happened consistently. The tabs visually appeared selected, but the content didn't update. This is a **significant functional bug** that makes half the garden inaccessible. ***

## 5. Three Top-Level Views Add More Confusion

Above the tabs, there are three icon buttons that switch between entirely different modes: [f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit](https://f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit.dev/garden)
- **Pen icon** → Your Garden (The Desk + 8 tabs)
- **Book icon** → Reading Room (others' work)
- **Bell/greenhouse icon** → Your Greenhouse (private tools)

These aren't labelled. As a first-time user, I had no idea what the icons meant until I clicked them. And the **Greenhouse** alone contains 7 more tools: Freewrite, Growth Journal, Inner Weather, Rituals, Compost, Reflections, and Circles. [f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit](https://f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit.dev/garden)

So the total feature count inside the Garden is roughly: **8 tabs + 3 modes + 7 greenhouse tools = 18 distinct areas.** That's a lot for a platform whose philosophy is "just write."

**Recommendation:** Label the three icons with text (even small text beneath). Collapse the Greenhouse tools into 2-3 essentials (Freewrite and Growth Journal are the strongest; Inner Weather, Rituals, Compost, and Reflections feel like nice-to-haves that could be merged or hidden).

***

## 6. The Writing Editor Is Actually Good

When I opened a piece ("The Cartography of Silence"), the editor itself was clean and focused: title at top, stage buttons (SEED → GROWING → READY), genre dropdown, tags, a simple rich-text toolbar, and the writing area. **This is the best part of the experience.** It's quiet, minimal, and lets you focus. [f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit](https://f46a60f2-86bc-410e-af69-29c0cbdde59c-00-1t11orrcucb62.spock.replit.dev/garden)

The one issue: the stage labels (Seed, Growing, Ready) plus "EXHIBITED" plus "PLANT" and "EXPORT" buttons on the card view add conceptual overhead. "Plant" in particular is confusing — does it publish? Move it to the gallery? The garden metaphor starts requiring a glossary.

**Recommendation:** Keep the editor as-is. Simplify the stage names to something more universal (Draft → In Progress → Done) or at minimum add a tooltip explaining what each stage means. Rename "Plant" to something clearer like "Submit" or "Share."

***

## 7. Filters on The Desk Show Wrong Counts

On The Desk, the filter bar showed "SEEDS 13" but when I clicked it, it displayed "No pieces match your search". The counts don't match the actual filtered results, which feels broken and erodes trust. ***

## 8. What Feels Unnecessary

| Feature | Why it's too much |
|---|---|
| **Rejection Wall** | Interesting concept but niche — most first-time users won't engage with this immediately |
| **The Press** | Unclear purpose, couldn't even load it |
| **Idea Drops** | Another idea capture tool when you already have Compost and the Desk |
| **Inner Weather** | Creative mood tracking is a nice idea but adds cognitive load |
| **Rituals** | Timed writing is available in many other apps; doesn't need its own section |
| **Reflections** | Overlaps with Growth Journal |
| **Compost** | Overlaps with the Desk itself (abandoned drafts can just live as seeds) |
| **The scroll-reveal animation** | Delays the actual experience |
| **The manifesto section** | Should be a separate page, not the landing page |

***

## Summary: How to Simplify

**The core idea is beautiful: write, and let the editors find you.** But the implementation has layered so many features, metaphors, and spaces on top of that simplicity that it contradicts the promise. Here's what I'd do:

1. **Landing page:** One tagline, 3-step explainer, one big "Enter" button. Move everything else to an About page.
2. **Garden entry:** Show The Desk only. One button: "+ New Piece." That's it.
3. **Progressive disclosure:** Unlock tabs/features as the writer engages (first piece → Workshop appears; first week → Tables; etc.)
4. **Merge overlapping tools:** Compost → just use Seeds on The Desk. Reflections → merge into Growth Journal. Rituals → add a timer to the Freewrite tool.
5. **Label everything:** The top icons need text labels. "Plant," "Bloom," "Seed" need tooltips or plain-English alternatives.
6. **Fix the bugs:** Tab switching, filter counts, and the scroll animation need attention before anything else.

The writing editor itself is the heart of the product — and it's genuinely good. Everything else should exist to serve it, not compete with it for attention.
